Home page

Introduction

Copper Basin is a beautiful area with amazing views and glacier lakes. This website will help you learn about the wildlife and terrain of Copper Basin. You will be able to see what terrain you need to prepare for, and what wildlife you can keep an eye out for.

Preparation

Copper Basin is a place of natural lanscapes and wildlife. Learning about these variable can help you be able to prepare in advance for situations that might catch you off gaurd otherwise.

Experience

This website is made by someone who grew up going to Copper Basin and has seen all it has to offer in terms of rough terrain, weather, and animals, so I can give you a good idea of what to expect and how to prepare.

Wildlife

Animal Species

Copper Basin has many kinds of wildlife like deer, elk, coyotes, mountain goats, bears, and wolves live in this area. The dense trees and mountains give these animals great places for shelter, hiding, and feeding spots.

Glacier Lakes

Copper basin is also home to many glacier lakes, these lakes are home to a variety of fish and provide water for the animals. The types of fish you can see are brook trout, rainbow trout, cutthoat, mountain whitefish, and the tiger trout.

Safety Precautions

The best way to be safe from the wildlife is to bring countermeasures like bear spray or something loud. You should also make sure to keep your food in a bear canister or hung up in a tree, and make sure to keep your campsite clean and free of food scraps.

Terrain

Differing Terrain

The terrain in Copper Basin ranges from steep rocky cliffs to nice flat walking paths. To be prepared for the terrain you will face I would always recommend to bring a good pair of hiking boots, if you're going during winter you should bring nice winter boots and snow pants. The snow can be up to 3 feet deep in some areas.

Weather

The time of year really affects how hard each path will be, in the summer/fall the snow will most likely be gone and the terrain for hiking paths will be fairly easy. In the winter/spring there will be quite a bit of snow and hiking paths will become a lot harder to navigate, you will want someone who knows the area if you're planning to go in the winter.

Hiking Trails

The type of terrain you will face depends heavily on what and where you are planning to go, if you are wanting to stay strictly to the trails and roads, they will be mostly well kept and wont have too difficult of obsticles for hikers.

Going off the Trails

If you want to just wander and climb to the tops of some of the ridges, you can expect it to have rocky, steep, and sometimes slippery slopes. Always be prepared to get lost by bringing a compass and a whistle. You should never go alone anywhere in the area, always bring a friend and have a radio to communicate just in case.
